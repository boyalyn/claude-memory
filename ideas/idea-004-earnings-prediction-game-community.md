## Hypothesis
People like to guess how a company's earnings report will turn out. A platform that turns this into a game (users vote /
"bet" on each upcoming earnings report), with content creators posting analysis and users posting their own content,
could grow into a community that people come back to every earnings season.
User's words (2026-10-24): "做一个关于赌财报的一个游戏化的投票的平台，然后我可能会有一些内容创作者，然后也有一些用户生成内容，做一个社群。"
Basis: the user's own idea, one conversation, no data.

Not specified yet (unknown, to be found in test):
- Real money, virtual points, or points that convert into prizes.
- What exactly is voted on (EPS/revenue beat vs miss, guidance up/down, stock move the next day, size of move).
- Target users and region / language (e.g. Chinese-speaking retail investors, Singapore, US).
- Which companies (US megacaps only, or wider).
- Where creators come from and why they would join (pay, audience, tools).

## How to test
- Data / tools needed: earnings calendar + actual results (free sources to be chosen); a cheap place to run polls (Telegram group / Discord / WeChat group, or a simple web page); a scoring rule (the forecast-journal Brier scoring could be reused); desk research on existing products.
- Steps (proposal, for the user to confirm):
  1. Desk research: who already does earnings prediction / voting / communities, how they make money, what users complain about. Candidates to check (from memory, unverified): Kalshi and Polymarket earnings-related markets, Estimize (crowd earnings estimates), Stocktwits sentiment, broker communities (moomoo/Futu 牛牛圈, Tiger, Webull, Robinhood prediction features).
  2. Legal check, before anything with money: rules for real-money betting on earnings in the target region (e.g. Singapore Gambling Control Act; US CFTC event contracts) vs a free points game. Record what each mode would require.
  3. Manual MVP for one earnings season (points only): a group where each week the user posts polls for 5-10 upcoming reports, a leaderboard after results, and 1-2 creators (or the user) post short previews.
  4. Measure: participants, votes per person per week, week-over-week return rate, number of user posts, creator post engagement.
- Success criterion (PROPOSAL, user to confirm before step 3): with at least 30 people who joined, at least 40% vote on 5 or more reports over the first 2 weeks, and at least 30% of first-week voters are still voting in week 4; at least 5 user posts per week without being asked.
- Failure criterion (PROPOSAL): fewer than 15% of joiners still voting in week 4, or almost no user-generated posts (fewer than 1 per week) after week 2.
- Rough cost: desk research ~2-3h; legal check ~1-2h of reading (a lawyer only if real money is pursued); manual MVP ~30 min per week for one earnings season (~4-6 weeks) + recruiting time; money ~S$0-50 (optional small ad spend to recruit).

## Money path (if any)
Unknown, to be found in test. Possible paths to check (not yet verified):
- Ads / sponsorship (brokers, finance apps); broker referral commissions.
- Premium subscription (creator analysis, stats, private leagues).
- Creator revenue share (paid posts, tips).
- Selling aggregated crowd forecasts as data (the Estimize model).
- Fees on real-money predictions, only if legally allowed in the target region.
Who pays: unknown. What can be lost: build and community-running time; legal exposure if real money is involved without a licence; recruiting / ad spend.

## Open questions (to test, not judgments)
- Points vs real money: which mode is legal in the target region, and does engagement differ? Test: legal check (step 2), then compare participation if both are ever possible.
- Is earnings season frequent enough to keep a community active between seasons (reports cluster in ~4-6 week windows each quarter)? Test: track activity in the gap weeks after the MVP season.
- Why would creators post here rather than on their existing platforms? Test: ask 5-10 finance creators what they would need (pay, audience, tools); count how many agree to post during the MVP.
- How do existing products (step 1 list) handle this, and what do their users say is missing? Evidence needed: product pages, pricing, reviews, user numbers where public.
- Does a leaderboard / scoring rule change behaviour (more votes, more return visits)? Test: compare weeks before and after the leaderboard is shown, within the MVP.
