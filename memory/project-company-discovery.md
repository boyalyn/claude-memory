User's framing (2026-10-12): wants a VC-like approach to spotting "young" versions of very large companies. Explicitly a long-term learning goal for a future startup, not short-term income (a change from the earlier money-only stance in [[feedback-goal-money-or-abandon]]). Said weekly predictions do not fit small companies (little changes in a week) and proposed adding failed companies to avoid survivorship bias.

Discussed, no experiment run: Bessembinder (4.3% of US stocks account for all net wealth creation 1926-2016), IPO/lottery-stock underperformance, gross profitability premium. Design points: cohort study defined at a point in time by observable features without looking at outcomes, then followed forward; failure defined in advance (bankruptcy, delisting for cause, -90%, low-price takeover; premium takeover counts as success); SEC XBRL filings as first-reported point-in-time data; free price data lacks delisted names (needs a source); luck cannot be measured for one firm, only versus a pure-luck compounding null model, return split into earnings growth vs valuation multiple change, persistence tests, and the share of outcomes predictable from ex-ante features; weekly habit becomes forming/updating theses, with resolution on quarterly earnings dates and slower questions.
Open decisions: build the point-in-time screen vs a watchlist and thesis journal; definition of failure; feature list. Related: [[project-forecast-journal]], [[project-forward-test]].
